It’s exactly 12:12pm when I start writing this. I’m at the La Villa des Abbesses cafe on the popular Rue des Abbesses.

A week ago, as my trip drew nearer, I had promised myself that I would write my next piece from out of a cafe in Paris and title it exactly that - “from a cafe in Paris”.

I arrived in Paris slightly over 24 hours ago on a 6am flight from London. The pilot had warned that it would be turbulent at the start of the flight and I had thought it would be the garden-variety turbulence. It wasn’t. It was the most turbulent flight I’d ever been on. The woman sat next to me on the flight looked visibly worried. Ironically, I was so sleepy from sleeping late the night before that I managed to get a 35-minute nap amidst it all.

On arriving, the Paris CDG made no impression on me. It felt like any other airport, it didn’t feel different at all. In that moment, I could’ve been in Gatwick or even Stansted. Nonetheless, I was optimistic the rest of my trip wouldn’t feel (indifferent) this way. I wanted difference, uniqueness, something to hold on to about the city.

My hotel check-in wasn’t until 2pm, so I had time to burn and I was going to do this by getting some of the items I needed to complete my outfit for the concert that originally brought me to Paris, then spend the rest of the time walking around the city centre.

Picture this: at the checkout counter of a fashion store in Châtelet Les Halles, I’m about to ask one of the staff if an item was available. I had started my ask in the usual style that worked for me in (French-speaking) Morocco: “Anglais? mon Francais petit”. The staff replied saying he didn’t speak English so I was about to walk away. A man, who appeared to be in his late 50s offered to help. He asked me to tell him what I wanted to say in English and he’d interpret in French.

After leaving the counter, I went to thank the man. I introduced myself and so did he. His name was Jean (not real name). Jean grew up in England before moving to Paris to complete his education. I asked him for recommendations on places to visit or things to do in the city, then he offered to show me around if I had time. If there was anything I had, it was the abundance of time. So I took his offer.

We stepped outside the mall where we had met and I was hit by a crazy cold wind. I told Jean the weather and the big bag I was carrying meant I couldn’t walk around at that time of the morning so we went to a cafe nearby. I like hearing people’s stories, so I wasn’t going to leave without hearing Jean’s. By this time, Paris was starting to feel different.

Over the next hour, Jean told me of how he moved to France, the Parisian people, how Paris compared to London, how people were friendlier in the South of France and his many travels. Copenhagen was Jean’s favorite city to visit, he spoke of how welcoming the city felt to him. Now, he was living in French Guyana. I also got a lesson in the Guyanas - up until I met Jean, I didn’t know there were 3 Guyanas, which means I didn’t know the Guyana I knew was the British one, and the other two were named French and Dutch Guyana appropriately. Somewhere during our conversation, my body started crying for more sleep, so I decided to head to my hotel. Jean paid for our coffee and we agreed to meet later in the day so he could show me the city as agreed.

I enjoyed listening to Jean and his stories, but I had fully planned to be on guard when we meet later in the day. The last time someone offered to show me a place, I was in Morocco travelling from Fes to Chefchauoen and he ended up priming me to be taken to a vendor in the medina who I later found out gave him a commission on the thing I bought at a price I’m not proud of - me, _a whole Lagos boy from Ikorodu oh!_  I wasn’t naive enough to not believe in stranger danger, but I also believe that our lives are fuller and happier if we don’t see it solely as an exercise in managing the risk of dying.

I met Jean again later in the evening. We talked a lot more. I asked him about his parents, if he still remembers their voice given how long ago they had died. At some point, we walked by a merch shop displaying postcards on the outside - I asked if he sent postcards when he was younger. He did. He also told me of how he did a lot of writing back then but hardly did any more of it these days. Jean spoke multiple languages and I got the impression that he was a big reader, so I asked about Albert Camus. It made sense to ask a French reader that old if they ever read Camus. He hadn’t read The Fall, which I jokingly judged him for, but he made up for it by having read The Stranger. We spoke about the impressions of absurdity from Camus’ works.

All of this conversation was happening as we walked from monument to monument. First stop was the Eiffel Tower, which I thought was an exceptional monument. I don’t exaggerate when I say I was in awe. It’s a beautiful work of engineering now, but even more impressive if you look at it through the lens of having been built in the 19th century.

After leaving the Eiffel, we went to Musée d’Orsay, only to find out it had closed just a few mins before we arrived. We then went to the Saint Michel area where there was the Notre Dame cathedral. It looked beautiful from the outside, but the queue to get inside the cathedral was too long, so I decided to skip. While in the area, we also made a stop at Saint Michel fountain. By this time, it was nearly 7pm and it was starting to get really cold again, so I offered to buy Jean a drink and we could call it a day. At the bar, we talked some more about Jean’s family life, his roots to Benin and the fact that his grandmother spoke Yoruba before finally going our separate ways.

Through all of this, Jean was very patient with my questions, he taught me a lot about Paris, and he tried his best to be a good makeshift photographer. I ended my evening by having dinner at a restaurant. The dinner was more spenny than I had budgeted for, but I should have known that it was going to be when I was told that the chef would cook it right in front of me. I enjoyed the food and the experience, though, so I can’t complain.

My headspace is back to my cafe now. Tonight the event I came for is finally happening. I’m very happy to be here, Paris is _different_.
